1

Hail at this address + Storm report

New

Why: the feature roofers name most in hail tools is a hail report for one address they can show the homeowner (HailTrace $40 a report, Interactive Hail Maps emails one per address). Roof Command already had the radar hail for every home and every storm; it only showed one number.

On the home record (This home): a bar per storm on file (13 since Mar 2024), 1 in line, the biggest hit, how old that storm is and its one-year mark (worded as a check: the policy decides). Storm report opens a branded page to print or save as PDF: storm-by-storm table with NWS size words (quarter, half dollar, golf ball), roof facts, neighbors on the same storm. No owner name, no price, no claim promises; radar estimate, not certified. Also in the record's More menu and the inbox home pane.

2

Neighbors who took the same hail

New

Why: the clearest canvassing complaint is tools that show where reps walked, not which doors deserve a knock. One booked inspection can become a street: the neighbors took the same storm.

Under the hail chart: how many homes within a quarter mile took 1 in+ on this home's biggest storm with no reroof permit since (343 for 1524 Yorkshire Dr), the nearest 12 with distance, hail and year built. Open one, or Add to today: it joins Today's list as "Neighbor, same storm".

3

At the door

New

Why: every canvassing tool has door outcomes, and roofers' most specific ask was that "Not home" lead somewhere (a return visit, a follow-up), not nowhere. The record already said "No phone on file: email or knock" but had no way to log a knock.

Four big buttons: Not home, Talked, Not interested, Book inspection. Each is logged on the home's history (so Activity, the board and the cloud copy see it) with Undo. Not home says when to come back (weekday evening, Saturday morning) and keeps the text draft one tap away (a person sends it). The record's Next task becomes "Knock again" and the pipeline card says "Knock again Tuesday after 5 pm". Talked moves a New hit to Contacted; Not interested parks it in Not now.

4

Storms opens on the hail

Fix

Why: Storms is the first screen in a demo. It opened on Plano and Allen, a grey map, while four of the five glowing hardest-hit neighbourhoods sat off screen in Rockwall.

Once the 6-month hardest-hit areas are known, the camera frames the top five (outliers dropped). It never moves a map the roofer already touched, a picked storm or an open list.

5

Materials tab

New

Why: Roofr and RoofSnap sell measurement reports with a material list, and roofers ask for a proposal that turns into a supplier order. The record already measures squares, pitch, waste and every edge.

A fifth tab beside Roof report: squares to order at the report's waste (17% for this hip roof), then bundles, starter, hip and ridge cap, underlayment, valley ice and water, drip edge and nails, each with the figure it came from. Common coverage numbers, labelled as such; boots and vents are counted on site. Copy list puts it on the clipboard for the supplier message the roofer writes.

6

Ask Nimbus: hail at an address

New

Why: the question a homeowner asks first is "did hail hit my house?". Nimbus now answers it for any address in the four counties, from the same radar the rest of the app reads, with the Storm report one tap away. New chip: Hail at an address.

7

Smaller wins and fixes

Polish

Home record opens on This home (next task, door, hail, roof) instead of a second copy of the pipeline board, and the 3D roof stays in view while the right column scrolls. First-morning guide on an empty inbox, desktop and phone. Calendar opened after working hours starts on the next working day (the phone day view was a column of "Past"), and the day view has Directions for the day (the day's inspections in booked order as one Maps route). Pipeline count: "50" no longer breaks into a boxed 5 over a 0 after it rolls.

R2

Round 2: the street around each home

New

Street hail map. Under the neighbor list and on the Storm report: one dot per home within 1,000 ft, coloured by the radar hail it took on that storm, reroofed-since homes hollow, the house ringed. 1524 Yorkshire Dr: 195 of 223 nearby homes took 1 in+ on Mar 25, 2025. On the homeowner's report it shows the street without any addresses.

Walk sheet. "Print a walk sheet" turns the neighbors into a one-page canvassing list: the nearest 40 doors with 1 in+ and no reroof since, in walking order, a map with the walking line, and boxes to tick at each door. Addresses only, never owner names.

Round 3: on Storms, pick a hardest-hit neighbourhood and "Print a walk sheet" builds the door list for that neighbourhood and that storm. The pipeline Filter has "Knock again (nobody home last time)". The record's More menu has "Walk sheet for this street".

Door knocks in Activity. Knocks get a door icon and their own Knocks filter, and stay out of Notes. Today's list rows no longer show a bare initial for owners not messaged yet.

Researched, not built (and why)

Certified hail reportsWe cannot certify weather. The Storm report says plainly it is a radar estimate.
Good / better / best pricing, e-sign, financingNeeds the roofer's own products and prices and a signing service. Showing them without real prices would be mock data.
Instant online quote widget for the roofer's websiteNeeds hosting on the roofer's site and their price list (Roofle charges $350 a month plus $2,000 setup for this).
Photo documentation (CompanyCam style)Needs a per-account photo store; the hosted build has no upload storage yet. A good next step once accounts sync.
Insurance claim and supplement trackingNo tool in the research does it well, and it needs claim data we do not have.
Territories, rep GPS, leaderboardsThe pilot is one small team. Worth it with several reps.
Push storm alerts to a phoneNeeds a phone app or push service; the cloud storm watch runs separately.
Door hangers, mailers, automated cold textsNever physical mail, and a person always taps Send (house rules).

Most screens are headless Chromium; the home record's 3D roof does not draw there, so the record views marked "real browser" (hail panel, door, Materials, Nimbus) were captured in the app's own browser pane at 800 by 500. Storms is a real GPU window. Every request leaving the laptop was blocked during the shots, and county owner files were blocked, so owner names show as initials or not at all. Phones and people are sample; homes, hail, roofs and permits are real records.
