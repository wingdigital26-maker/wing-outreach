Bet 3: the Homeowner Proof Page

A per-home public storm record a rep sends by hand in a text or email: radar hail at the address, the nearest National Weather Service hail report, the street on that storm, re-roof permits nearby (counts only), roof age and the LiDAR size estimate, the roofer, a "Book a free inspection" button and a consent box that records written consent on the home's record. Branch ui/proofpage, built 2026-10-23.

Before any cold text goes out (legal stop signs).
  • Scrub phones against the National Do Not Call Registry and the Texas no-call list: DNC rules cover human-tapped texts too. The app has no DNC flag yet.
  • A Texas lawyer on Bus. & Com. Code 305.001 (sales calls to mobile numbers need consent) and the ch. 302 registration exemption (302.059).
  • A backend for consent and opt-out records (today they live on one device), kept 5 years.
  • Jackson's mailing address and a privacy policy link on file (emails are held until the address is there).

The pitch: every template

Rendered live from lib/rc-proof.js with sample facts, exactly what the dialog drafts. The outlined card in each group is the one I would lead with. Gate "warm" = only after a reply, consent on the page, an opt-in or a past customer; cold follow-ups go by email.

Why these leads. Facts: carriers filter texts with public link shorteners, a domain you own is the fix (Twilio, Bird); links get extra carrier scrutiny and perform when expected (Drips); no independent roofing SMS reply-rate study exists, the vendor numbers (38%, 32.7%) are unaudited. Opinion: lead cold texts with B (ask first, link on yes), which keeps the 10-09 no-link rule and turns the link into something the homeowner asked for; email carries the link from the first touch; test A and C against B at 100 sends each once the link is on the roofer's domain.
